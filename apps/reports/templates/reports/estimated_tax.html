As a sole proprietor you pay income tax and self-employment tax on the shop's profit four times a year: federal Form 1040-ES and New York IT-2105, due on the same days. Owner's draws are not an expense and don't change the tax. This is a set-aside guide from the shop's own figures, not a calculation of what you owe. The tax columns are on the profit to date; "Pay now" is that estimate less what the earlier periods asked for.

How it is worked out

  • Profit: net sales less cost of goods, postage, payment fees, stock lost and samples, from the margin report. Expenses kept only in Wave (hosting, software, supplies, the home office) are not in it: subtract them, or the estimate runs high.
  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of the profit (the 12.4% Social Security part only up to {{ wage_base|usd }} of earnings).
  • Income tax: the profit less half the self-employment tax, at {{ federal_rate|floatformat:"-1" }}% federal and {{ state_rate|floatformat:"-1" }}% New York. These rates are placeholders (ESTIMATED_TAX_FEDERAL_RATE and ESTIMATED_TAX_STATE_RATE): set them with your tax preparer from your other income.
  • No penalty applies if this year's payments reach last year's total tax (110% of it if your income was over $150,000), so paying a quarter of that each period is a safe alternative. Due dates on a weekend or holiday move to the next working day.
